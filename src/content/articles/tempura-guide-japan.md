## Who This Guide Is For

This is for anyone deciding between a quick tendon lunch and a proper sit-down tempura meal, and for anyone curious why the same basic dish can range from a 900-yen bowl to a multi-course counter experience.

## How These Compare

- **Speed and format** move from a fast, single-bowl meal at a tendon shop to a slower, piece-by-piece counter experience at a specialty restaurant or omakase counter.
- **Oil and technique** shift from standard, efficiency-focused frying at casual chains to sesame-oil frying and individual timing at specialty restaurants.
- **Price** scales accordingly, from under 1,000 yen for a casual tendon bowl to significantly more for a full omakase course.

> Specific restaurant recommendations, prices, and seasonal menu items change over time — treat the above as a framework for understanding the format rather than a guarantee of current offerings at any one restaurant.

## Practical Tips

- Tempura is meant to be eaten promptly after frying, while the coating is still crisp — this is part of why counter-style restaurants serve piece by piece rather than all at once.
- A light dipping sauce (tentsuyu) with grated daikon is common at specialty restaurants, as an alternative to eating tempura plain or with salt.
- If offered a choice of salt or sauce for a specific piece, trying it with salt first is a reasonable way to taste the ingredient itself before adding sauce.
