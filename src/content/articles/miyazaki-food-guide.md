  - question: What exactly is chicken nanban?
    answer: >-
      Fried chicken dipped in a sweet-and-sour vinegar sauce and topped with a creamy, egg-based tartar sauce. It was invented in Nobeoka, in Miyazaki Prefecture, in the mid-1950s, and later refined into its now-familiar tartar-topped form by a restaurant in Miyazaki City before spreading nationwide.
  - question: Is Miyazaki beef the same thing as Wagyu?
    answer: >-
      Yes — it's a regional wagyu brand, similar in concept to Kobe beef, with its own grading and branding standards specific to cattle raised in Miyazaki Prefecture.

## Who This Guide Is For

This is for anyone whose Japan itinerary includes Miyazaki (or Kyushu more broadly) and wants to eat like a local rather than search for whatever comes up first online. It's not a comprehensive "best restaurants in Miyazaki" list — it's the specific handful of things worth prioritizing if you're only in town for a day or two.

## Practical Tips

- Miyazaki City is small enough to cover on foot or by rental bicycle; you won't need a car just to eat well.
- Cash is still useful at smaller, older spots like the ones above, even though card acceptance has improved in recent years.
- If you can only do one sit-down meal, pair chicken nanban with a walk through the Ichiban-gai arcade in central Miyazaki, where several of the city's older restaurants are clustered.

> Restaurant hours, prices, and availability change over time — treat the specifics above as a starting point to verify closer to your trip, not a guarantee.
