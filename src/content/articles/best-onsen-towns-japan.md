## Who This Trip Is Best For

This is for travelers who want to build real downtime into a Japan itinerary rather than treating every day as a sightseeing checklist. It also rewards a bit of range — the towns here span a busy Tokyo day-trip (Hakone) all the way to a secluded mountain cluster reached only by shuttle bus (Nyuto Onsenkyo).

## Best Time to Visit

- **Winter** is arguably the classic onsen season — the contrast between hot water and cold air, plus snow-covered scenery at places like Ginzan Onsen and Noboribetsu, is hard to beat.
- **Autumn** foliage pairs especially well with mountain onsen areas like Nyuto Onsenkyo.
- Onsen bathing itself is a year-round activity — there's no real off-season, just different scenery depending on when you go.

## Suggested Route

A realistic onsen-focused addition to a first or second Japan trip:

1. **Near Tokyo (1-2 nights):** Hakone or Kusatsu, depending on whether Fuji views or mineral-rich water matters more to you.
2. **Kansai add-on (1-2 nights):** Kinosaki Onsen, especially if you're already routing through Kyoto or Osaka toward the Sea of Japan coast.
3. **A dedicated onsen trip:** Tohoku's Ginzan Onsen and Nyuto Onsenkyo, or Kyushu's Beppu, work best as the anchor of their own regional trip rather than a quick add-on.

## Practical Tips

- Bring your own small towel if your ryokan doesn't provide one for the walk between bathhouses — some public baths charge a small fee for a rental towel.
- Most onsen require full nudity in the bathing area itself — swimsuits are generally not permitted in traditional baths, though some newer or mixed-use facilities are an exception.
- If you have tattoos, it's worth checking a specific onsen's policy before you arrive rather than assuming either way. See our [onsen etiquette guide](/onsen/onsen-etiquette-guide-japan/) for the full rundown.

> Ryokan availability, bathhouse hours, and specific policies (tattoos, day-use access) change over time and by establishment — treat the above as a starting point to confirm closer to your trip.

## Where to Stay

For most of these towns, staying at a ryokan directly in the onsen district is the point — the walk between bathhouses, the yukata, and the kaiseki dinner are as much a part of the experience as the bathing itself. Booking a ryokan with its own private or exclusive bath is worth the premium if soaking without limited public hours matters to you.

## Things to Book

- **Ryokan reservations**, especially for smaller, well-known spots like Ginzan Onsen and Nyuto Onsenkyo, which have limited rooms and can book out weeks ahead for peak season.
- **Regional transportation**, since several of these towns (Nyuto Onsenkyo especially) require a specific bus or shuttle connection beyond the main train line.
