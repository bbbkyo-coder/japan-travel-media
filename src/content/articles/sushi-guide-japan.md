## Who This Guide Is For

This is for anyone trying to figure out which kind of sushi meal actually fits a given moment on a trip — a quick, affordable lunch, a proper sit-down dinner, or a genuine special-occasion splurge — rather than treating all sushi restaurants as interchangeable.

## How These Compare

- **Price** rises sharply from kaiten-zushi to Edomae to omakase, and this generally does track a real difference in ingredient sourcing and preparation time, though not always in a linear way.
- **Format** shifts from self-serve or app-ordered at kaiten-zushi, to counter service with some menu choice at Edomae restaurants, to a fully chef-directed sequence at omakase.
- **Time commitment** also scales up, from a quick in-and-out kaiten-zushi meal to a two-hour-plus omakase sitting.

> Specific restaurant recommendations, prices, and reservation requirements change over time — treat the above as a framework for understanding the format, and research current specifics for any particular restaurant you are set on.

## Practical Tips

- It is completely normal to eat sushi with your hands rather than chopsticks, especially at more traditional Edomae counters.
- Soy sauce is meant to touch the fish side of a piece, not the rice, to avoid oversaturating the rice with sauce.
- Ginger (gari) between pieces is meant as a palate cleanser, not a topping to eat alongside the sushi itself.
