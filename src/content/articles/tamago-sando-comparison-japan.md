## Who This Comparison Is For

This is for anyone who wants to understand why a "boring" sandwich has such a devoted following in Japan, and who wants to actually taste the range of what tamago sando can be rather than trying just one version and assuming that's the whole story.

## How These Compare

These versions sit on a rough spectrum from fast-and-cheap to slow-and-indulgent:

- **Convenience store (7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart):** The everyday version. Cheap, consistent, available anywhere, and a genuinely good introduction.
- **Depachika:** A step up in ingredient quality and variety, still grab-and-go, found in department store basements.
- **Komeda Coffee (bonus pick):** Not about luxury, but about value — a generously sized sandwich in a genuinely comfortable place to sit and eat it, without rushing.
- **Kissaten:** A sit-down experience built around atmosphere and unhurried coffee as much as the sandwich itself.
- **Wako Ginza:** The luxury end — a dessert-like treat rather than a meal, in a class of its own.

> Prices, exact ingredients, and availability change over time and by location. Treat the descriptions above as general character sketches of each style rather than a guarantee of what you'll get on any specific visit.

## Practical Tips

- If you want to do a genuine side-by-side comparison, buy the three convenience store versions on the same day — this is a common, low-effort way locals settle the debate themselves.
- Convenience store tamago sando is refrigerated and meant to be eaten same-day; it's a reliable, portable option for train rides and quick lunches while sightseeing.
- For the Wako Ginza experience, treat it as a planned stop rather than something to fit in on the way to somewhere else.
